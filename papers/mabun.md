# Mabûn: A Circulation-Based Economic Model for Stability in Non-Equilibrium Systems

## Abstract

This paper introduces **Mabûn**, the economic layer of the Zanistarast framework. It rejects the classical assumption of perfect equilibrium and instead proposes:

> Economic stability emerges from continuous circulation, not static balance.

Building on non-equilibrium thermodynamics, network theory, and agent-based economics, we argue that:

- perfect justice is unattainable  
- equilibrium is unstable  
- circulation is the only sustainable stabilizing mechanism  

We formalize circulation mathematically and propose simulation-based validation.

---

## 1. Introduction

Classical economics assumes:

- equilibrium markets  
- rational agents  
- optimal distribution  

However, real-world systems show:

- inequality persists  
- shocks disrupt balance  
- static equilibrium collapses  

Mabûn proposes a different principle:

> Stability is not balance. Stability is **flow**.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Economy = Circulation of resources across agents over time**

Not:

- fixed distribution  
- static fairness  

But:

- dynamic redistribution  

---

## 3. Why Perfect Justice is Impossible

Constraints:

- time variation  
- spatial inequality  
- production differences  
- information asymmetry  

Thus:

> Perfect equality cannot be sustained.

---

## 4. Circulation Model

We define resource evolution:

\[
R_i(t+1) = (1 - \alpha) R_i(t) + \alpha \bar{R}(t)
\]

Where:

- \(R_i\) = resources of agent i  
- \(\bar{R}\) = average resource  
- \(\alpha\) = circulation rate  

---

## 5. Interpretation

- \(\alpha = 0\) → no redistribution → collapse  
- \(\alpha = 1\) → forced equality → instability  
- optimal \(\alpha\) → dynamic balance  

---

## 6. System Behavior

### Without circulation:

- wealth accumulates  
- inequality increases  
- system destabilizes  

### With circulation:

- extremes reduce  
- stability emerges  
- resilience increases  

---

## 7. Expansion vs Constraint

All agents:

> attempt to expand their resource space

But:

- unlimited expansion → conflict  
- constraint + circulation → sustainability  

---

## 8. Natural Analogy

In nature:

- water cycles  
- nutrients circulate  
- energy flows  

No system is static.

Thus:

> Economy must mimic ecological circulation.

---

## 9. Ethical Layer

Mabûn introduces:

> Circulation as ethical necessity

Not charity, but:

- system survival mechanism  

---

## 10. Infrastructure Model

Certain systems must be non-circulatory (shared):

- water  
- energy  
- health  
- education  

These act as:

> stabilizing base layers

---

## 11. Production Allocation Model

Instead of taxation-only systems:

- production zones allocated  
- output directed to public systems  

Example:

- agriculture → education funding  
- energy → healthcare  

---

## 12. Simulation Model

Agents:

- produce  
- consume  
- exchange  

System tracks:

- inequality (Gini coefficient)  
- stability  
- collapse threshold  

---

## 13. Hypotheses

H1:

> Systems without circulation collapse.

H2:

> Moderate circulation produces stability.

H3:

> Over-centralization reduces adaptability.

---

## 14. Falsifiability

Mabûn fails if:

- static systems outperform circulation  
- inequality does not destabilize systems  
- circulation does not improve resilience  

---

## 15. Integration with Zanistarast

- Hebûn → existence  
- Zanabûn → knowledge  
- Mabûn → movement (economy)  

Thus:

> Economy is the **energy flow of existence**

---

## 16. Conclusion

Mabûn establishes:

> Stability is not achieved through equality, but through circulation.

This allows:

- real-world modeling  
- simulation  
- policy testing

# Mabûn: The Theory of Economic Circulation in Zanistarast Framework

## Abstract

This paper introduces **Mabûn**, the economic layer of the Zanistarast framework. It redefines economy not as accumulation or equilibrium, but as **dynamic circulation of resources across time, space, and production conditions**.

Mabûn rejects the possibility of perfect justice and static balance, proposing instead a system where **ethical circulation approximates justice under real-world constraints**. This model integrates ontology, ecology, and ethics into a unified economic theory.

---

## 1. Introduction

Traditional economic systems are built on:

- accumulation (capitalism)  
- redistribution (socialism)  
- equilibrium models  

However, these frameworks fail to fully address:

- dynamic resource variation  
- ecological constraints  
- moral responsibility  
- impossibility of perfect justice  

Mabûn introduces a different principle:

> **Economy is circulation, not accumulation.**

---

## 2. Core Definition

Mabûn is defined as:

> **The dynamic circulation of resources across time, space, and production frequency**

Formally:

\[
Flow(R, t, x) = \text{circulation of resources over time and space}
\]

Where:

- \(R\) = resources  
- \(t\) = time  
- \(x\) = spatial distribution  

---

## 3. Rejection of Perfect Justice

Mabûn explicitly states:

- Perfect justice is impossible  
- Perfect balance is impossible  

Because:

- beings expand their existence  
- resources vary across time and space  
- mortality and limitation exist  

Thus:

> Justice is approximated through circulation, not achieved through static equality.

---

## 4. Principle of Circulation

Instead of accumulation:

- resources must **flow**  
- stagnation leads to imbalance  
- hoarding leads to systemic collapse  

Inspired by ecological systems:

- trees consume what is given to them  
- animals do not consume everything  
- each being operates within its fıtrat  

> Circulation aligns economy with nature.

---

## 5. Distribution Logic

Resource distribution depends on:

- **time** → availability cycles  
- **space** → geographical constraints  
- **production frequency** → supply variation  

Thus:

\[
R_i = f(t, x, production)
\]

There is no fixed distribution rule.

> Distribution is contextual, not absolute.

---

## 6. Human Responsibility

Humans differ from other beings:

- possess will  
- possess intelligence  
- possess moral responsibility  

Thus:

> Humans must regulate circulation through ethics.

Each human society:

- functions as an ecosystem  
- has unique conditions  
- requires adaptive circulation models  

---

## 7. Ethical Safeguards

Even in free systems:

- unrestricted markets can produce imbalance  
- therefore **ethical correction mechanisms are required**

Example principles:

- social protection mechanisms (e.g. zakat-like systems)  
- reduction of unnecessary monetary loops  
- prioritization of essential services  

Goal:

> Protect society while maintaining circulation.

---

## 8. Public Infrastructure Model

Certain systems should evolve toward **non-monetary access**:

- healthcare  
- education  
- transportation  
- essential utilities  

Funding model:

- not pure taxation  
- but **resource-linked production systems**

Example:

- agricultural land → funds education  
- production chains → sustain public systems  

> Economy becomes a **support system for society**, not an extraction system.

---

## 9. Systemic Model

Mabûn integrates with:

- **Hebûn** → existence and potential  
- **Zanabûn** → knowledge and validation  
- **Rasterast** → governance and ethics  

AI role:

- simulate flows  
- detect imbalance  
- optimize circulation  

> AI assists, but humans define ethics.

---

## 10. Conclusion

Mabûn redefines economy as:

- not accumulation  
- not equilibrium  
- not static justice  

> Economy is **dynamic, ethical circulation under constraint**.

This model:

- aligns with nature  
- accepts limitation  
- enables sustainable systems
